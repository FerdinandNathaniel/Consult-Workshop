# Executive Summary

Regulatory momentum is shifting decisively toward publisher and government oversight of AI systems, with two major jurisdictions implementing substantive controls on model deployment and data usage. The UK Competition and Markets Authority has mandated that Google grant publishers explicit opt-out rights for AI Search features and model fine-tuning, establishing a precedent for content creator agency in AI value chains. Concurrently, the Trump administration signed an executive order establishing a voluntary pre-release vetting framework for frontier AI models, signaling that even market-oriented policymakers now view government coordination on AI safety as compatible with innovation. These parallel developments—one focused on data governance and the other on model security—reflect a convergence around the principle that unfettered AI deployment carries unacceptable risks to both market competition and critical infrastructure. The OECD's release of its AI Policy Toolkit underscores that this regulatory turn is not isolated but part of a coordinated international effort to translate AI governance principles into actionable policy frameworks.

[Yann LeCun](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2061837853944848640) argued that AI will become the primary interface layer for computing and must be open-source with privacy-first design, warning that "if a closed source solution wins this layer, it's a disaster for the world"—particularly if controlled by a single company. This reflects growing policy concerns about concentrated corporate control over foundational AI infrastructure and its implications for digital sovereignty and user privacy.
