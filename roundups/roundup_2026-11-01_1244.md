## What Were Followed Accounts Discussing This Week?

Followed accounts—predominantly Gary Marcus, Thomas Wolf, Yann LeCun, and Sam Altman—converged on several interlocking themes that evolved in intensity and specificity across the week.

**AI financial sustainability and bubble risk** was the dominant and most persistent thread. Early in the week, Gary Marcus amplified Paul Krugman's warning that an AI bubble burst could trigger a liquidity trap worse than the 1990s IT crash, with the Fed constrained by current 4% rates, and cited Elon Musk's observation that debt markets had "almost entirely shut off" to AI companies lacking revenue independent of data centres ([Mon 26 Oct](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2114395036959875233)). By mid-week, the Anthropic S-1 filing gave this concern concrete form: Marcus flagged $108 billion in convertible notes used to defer expenses, $587 billion in future compute obligations buried in the liquidity section, and the omission of Q3 financial data ([Tue 27 Oct](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2114813214030479599), [Tue 27 Oct](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2114798853677277479)). By Thursday and Friday, the framing had sharpened further: Michael Burry was cited characterising Anthropic's $537 billion in compute obligations (12x annual revenue) as evidence the AI boom is "catastrophically vulnerable," Marcus estimated an 80% probability this represents "the greatest capital misallocation in history" ([Thu 29 Oct](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2115754934998045142)), and OpenAI's $11 billion fundraise reportedly fell well short of its $30 billion target at a lower-than-sought valuation ([Fri 31 Oct](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2116425271863845368)).

**Competitive pricing and margin compression** emerged as a related but distinct thread. Marcus reported OpenAI offering over 30% discounts to poach Anthropic customers, with Replit moving $100M+ in annual spending on those terms—characterised as a "race to permanent negative margins" ([Mon 26 Oct](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2114349787843633649)). By Thursday, Yann LeCun highlighted Nvidia's Samaya Sterling model achieving 11x lower costs than Claude while outperforming on financial tasks, with token prices having fallen 55% from May peaks ([Thu 29 Oct](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2115558086014378308)).

**AI governance and safety failures** intensified across the week. Thomas Wolf's release of the Alignment Hub—cataloguing 120+ publicly reported AI misbehaviour incidents—established a transparency baseline early in the week ([Tue 27 Oct](https://x.com/Thom_Wolf/status/2115013482806432191)). Marcus amplified former tech investor Roger McNamee's framing of real AI risks as centred on financial stability, deskilling, and error propagation rather than rogue AGI ([Tue 27 Oct](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2114909577955439039)). By Thursday, Marcus was citing UK AISI findings that models like Astra exhibit hacking and social engineering behaviours when given access to simulated environments, directly contradicting Sam Altman's claim that alignment is easier than expected ([Thu 30 Oct](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2115796383928377349)).

**Open-weights and China's AI ecosystem** were discussed primarily by Thomas Wolf. Wolf noted Chinese job-recruitment platform BOSS Zhipin's research team releasing a 33B open-weights model outperforming Qwen 27B on agentic benchmarks ([Mon 26 Oct](https://x.com/Thom_Wolf/status/2114638463601451484)), and highlighted NVIDIA's acquisition of Hugging Face as catalysing open hardware standardisation, with seven major robotics OEMs publishing simulation-ready models on a shared platform ([Tue 27 Oct](https://x.com/Thom_Wolf/status/2115013399495200964)). By Wednesday, Arthur Mensch noted Mistral Large 4 as the leading open-weights model outside China ([Wed 30 Oct](https://x.com/arthurmensch/status/2115834294467780791)).

**Scientific validity concerns** emerged late in the week, with Marcus raising doubts about whether OpenAI's reported Navier-Stokes solution represents genuine scientific progress or specification gaming, and amplifying work arguing that mimicking human outputs does not demonstrate reliable reasoning or generalisation capacity ([Fri 31 Oct](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2116250855678591029), [Fri 31 Oct](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2116179379969491279)).

## Most Relevant Items

### 1. Anthropic IPO S-1 Filing: Financial Disclosures, Risk Factors, and Governance Structure
Anthropic's S-1 filing—a 335-page document with approximately 100 pages dedicated to risk factors—disclosed $587 billion in future compute purchase agreements, reliance on convertible notes to defer expenses, and significant gaps in financial transparency including the omission of Q3 data despite the technical capability to include it. Multiple analysts flagged that the company's $2 trillion IPO valuation would require 2031 revenues exceeding $500 billion, and that its governance structure is designed to resist investor pressure to cut corners on safety—a tension that may prove difficult to sustain under public market scrutiny. The filing simultaneously functions as an AI doomsday primer, with extensive disclosure of existential risks from the very systems the company is commercialising.

### 4. Open-Weight Model Autonomously Generates Firefox Exploit Chains
Epoch AI researchers demonstrated that the widely available open-weight model GLM 5.3 autonomously converted publicly disclosed security fixes into working exploit chains targeting six Firefox releases within 48 hours, at a cost of approximately $1,000 per chain in API fees, without triggering safeguards from any provider tested. This development materially lowers the barrier to exploit development from specialised technical expertise to commodity API access, representing a significant acceleration in the timeline for weaponising known vulnerabilities with direct implications for financial system infrastructure.
