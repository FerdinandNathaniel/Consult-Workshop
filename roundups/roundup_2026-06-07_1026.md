## Summary of the Week

Regulatory momentum on AI accelerated across multiple jurisdictions, with the UK Competition and Markets Authority mandating that [Google grant publishers opt-out rights over AI Search features and model fine-tuning](https://www.theverge.com/tech/942302/google-search-ai-overviews-uk-cma-publisher-opt-out), while President Trump signed a [new executive order establishing a voluntary pre-release vetting framework for frontier AI models](https://www.theverge.com/policy/941775/trump-ai-executive-order)—a framework that [OpenAI promptly diverged from by publishing its own regulatory proposal](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/06/03/openai-white-house-ai-safety-rules-00948478). AI governance tensions extended to capital markets and public ownership, as [Anthropic confidentially filed for what could be the largest IPO in history](https://the-decoder.com/claude-maker-anthropic-files-for-ipo-with-the-sec/) while the Trump administration simultaneously opened [negotiations with OpenAI over a direct government equity stake](https://the-decoder.com/openai-and-the-trump-administration-are-negotiating-a-government-stake-in-the-ai-startup/), raising "too big to fail" concerns among analysts. The week also surfaced concrete AI security risks, with [Anthropic's Mythos model reportedly deployed in NSA offensive cyber operations](https://the-decoder.com/anthropics-mythos-model-is-reportedly-powering-nsa-offensive-cyber-ops-against-china-and-iran/) and a [Meta AI customer support agent exploited to steal Instagram accounts](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/06/05/1138437/the-meta-hack-shows-theres-more-to-ai-security-than-mythos/), underscoring that governance gaps carry immediate operational consequences.

## What Were Followed Accounts Discussing This Week?

Followed accounts—predominantly Gary Marcus, with contributions from Sam Altman, Yann LeCun, and Andrew Ng—coalesced around four recurring themes that intensified across the week.

**AI financial sustainability and market structure** was the dominant thread. Marcus argued early in the week that the AI sector's winner-take-all framing is technically unfounded, predicting [commodity pricing and industry-wide financial strain](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2061617157897912370), and warned that [2026 would be remembered as the year retail investors were left holding the bag](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2061505704503206107) as frontier labs rush to IPO. By mid-week, he sharpened this into a structural critique: [IBM CEO Arvind Krishna's claim that the industry needs $6–8 trillion in capex](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2062038461234946418) but would require $1–2 trillion in annual new revenue to break even was characterised as "hard to make square." By week's end, Marcus was warning that [OpenAI's search for government investment signals an absence of a credible path to profitability](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2062998693364764788), and that [proposed U.S. government stakes in AI companies risk replicating the Huawei distrust dynamic globally](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2063119012461195624).

**AI hype and capability claims** were persistently scrutinised. Marcus challenged anthropomorphic framings of AI systems throughout the week, and by Thursday was specifically critiquing [Anthropic's recursive self-improvement claims as a definitional "bait and switch"](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2062689785274364027) on AGI. He also documented [prior failed employment disruption predictions by Hinton and Amodei](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2062403373387939853) as evidence of systematic hype rather than rigorous analysis.

**AI governance and public benefit** evolved from abstract debate to concrete policy proposals. Sam Altman countered wealth-distribution concerns by highlighting [OpenAI Foundation's $130M+ in grants across biosecurity, cybersecurity, and safety](https://x.com/sama/status/2061562575322492937). Marcus acknowledged the tension between private investment claims and IP theft concerns, suggesting [Senator Sanders' proposed AI Sovereign Wealth Fund has merit](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2061532601492652494). By week's end, Marcus and others were engaging directly with [Andrew Yang's proposal to tax AI systems](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2062584645255057782) as a mechanism for compensating unremunerated intellectual property.

**AI safety and biosecurity** emerged as a cross-cutting concern. Sam Altman highlighted [OpenAI's biodefense initiatives](https://x.com/sama/status/2061101875303530871) early in the week, and by Thursday a letter co-signed by Altman, Dario Amodei, and Demis Hassabis was [urging Congress to mandate screening of synthetic DNA orders](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2062587529296007427), with AI systems reportedly already outperforming PhD-level virologists on lab procedures. Yann LeCun added a distinct governance concern, warning that [Trump administration political vetting of science funding](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2062917133873414187) represents a departure from peer-review meritocracy with long-run consequences for research quality.

### 3. UK CMA Forces Google to Grant Publishers Control Over AI Search Content
The UK Competition and Markets Authority imposed a conduct rule requiring Google to allow website owners to opt out of having their content used in AI Overviews and for fine-tuning AI models. The ruling establishes a regulatory precedent for publisher agency in AI value chains and represents one of the most concrete competition enforcement actions against AI-integrated search to date. It signals that data governance—specifically who controls content inputs to AI systems—is becoming a live enforcement priority for competition authorities.
