## What Were Followed Accounts Discussing This Week?

Discourse among followed accounts was dominated by **Gary Marcus** and **Yann LeCun**, with occasional contributions from **Thomas Wolf** and others. Several themes recurred and intensified across the week.

**AI economics and valuation sustainability** was the most persistent thread. Early in the week, LeCun flagged Wharton research suggesting AI companies must achieve 2.7x productivity gains—an unprecedented rate—or face bankruptcy, contextualising reports of government bailout discussions ([Yann LeCun via X, 9 Jun](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2064041550527508785)). By mid-week, Marcus was drawing explicit parallels between OpenAI and WeWork, pointing to SoftBank's failed loan attempt and reported plans for drastic price cuts as evidence of structural financial weakness with downstream implications for Nvidia, Oracle, and CoreWeave ([Gary Marcus via X, 11 Jun](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2064927386706014607)). By Thursday, the concern had broadened: Cisco's Jayesh Patel (amplified by Marcus) warned that token costs are "far higher than the actual value that these tokens are generating at scale," and analysts were comparing hyperscaler capex trajectories to the telecom bubble ([Gary Marcus via X, 12 Jun](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2065201239433076791)).

**AI industry credibility and corporate accountability** was a second recurring theme. Marcus challenged OpenAI's unfulfilled commitments to compensate artists and writers ([Gary Marcus via X, 9 Jun](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2064139168809402433)), characterised Anthropic and OpenAI safety messaging as "pre-IPO marketing" ([Gary Marcus via X, 9 Jun](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2064167104845422898)), and noted Jensen Huang's refusal to testify under oath on Nvidia's China sales ([Gary Marcus via X, 9 Jun](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2064132381246685483)). Anthropic's mid-week policy reversal—walking back a rule that would have covertly limited Claude's ability to assist in developing competing AI models—reinforced the credibility concerns ([Wired, 11 Jun](https://www.wired.com/story/anthropic-responds-to-backlash-on-claudes-secret-sabotage-on-ai-research/)).

**Regulatory dynamics and government intervention** evolved significantly across the week. Early discussion centred on the risk of light-touch US regulation backfiring after a major incident ([Gary Marcus via X, 8 Jun](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2063851282603843722)). By Thursday, the conversation had shifted to the actual mechanics of intervention: Marcus flagged a New York AG subpoena targeting OpenAI on advertising, user engagement, data handling, and model sycophancy ([Gary Marcus via X, 13 Jun](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2065667991258505559)), while Thomas Wolf travelled to Washington to advocate for open-source AI as an alternative to concentrated corporate control ([Thomas Wolf via X, 13 Jun](https://x.com/Thom_Wolf/status/2065714252678152234)). Marcus also criticised White House efforts to attach federal AI preemption clauses to children's safety legislation, characterising it as a de facto industry bailout ([Gary Marcus via X, 12 Jun](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2065152587373084692)).

**Open-source and multi-model architectures** as a counterweight to frontier model dominance was a consistent LeCun theme. He highlighted Stanford research showing local models now answer 71.3% of real-world queries accurately (up from 23.2% in 2023) at a fraction of cost and energy ([Yann LeCun via X, 9 Jun](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2064082422010925178)), and argued that Western competitiveness depends on distributed, open access to research and compute rather than closed acceleration ([Yann LeCun via X, 11 Jun](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2064671197904728160)). ## Most Relevant Items

**1. US government forces global shutdown of Anthropic's Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5**

The US government ordered Anthropic to disable global access to its two most advanced models, citing jailbreak vulnerabilities. Anthropic complied but pushed back publicly, arguing the vulnerabilities are minor, exist in competing systems including GPT-5.5, and that the precedent could effectively halt all frontier model deployments industry-wide—an ironic outcome given Anthropic's own prior emphasis on Mythos cybersecurity risks. This is the most direct government enforcement action against a frontier AI provider to date.

*Surfaced: Fri 13 Jun · [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/us-government-forces-anthropic-to-disable-claude-fable-5-and-mythos-5-for-all-customers-worldwide/) · [Wired](https://www.wired.com/story/anthropic-says-us-government-ordered-it-to-shut-down-mythos-models/)*

**2. EU invokes emergency powers to compel Meta to reopen WhatsApp to rival AI assistants**

The European Commission issued interim measures—only the second deployment of this power in two decades—ordering Meta to restore free WhatsApp infrastructure access for rival AI chatbot providers while its antitrust investigation continues. The Commission determined that without immediate action, competitors would suffer serious and irreparable harm in the emerging general-purpose AI assistant market. The intervention signals the EU's readiness to use its most forceful tools to prevent dominant platforms from leveraging existing positions to entrench advantages in adjacent AI markets.

*Surfaced: Wed 10 Jun · [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/947516/meta-whatsapp-eu-third-party-ai-chatbot-ban-order) · [Politico EU](https://www.politico.eu/article/eu-orders-meta-to-reopen-whatsapp-to-ai-rivals-for-free/)*

**3. Germany establishes AI Safety Institute (DE-AISI) modelled on UK's AISI**

Germany's National Security Council approved the creation of DE-AISI to test frontier models from Anthropic and OpenAI for security risks, following the British institutional template. The briefing's analysis flags a structural vulnerability: as long as the EU lacks indigenous frontier AI capabilities, both DE-AISI and its UK counterpart remain dependent on US providers whose operations are embedded within American national security frameworks—limiting European policy autonomy even as institutional capacity improves.

*Surfaced: Thu 11 Jun · [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/germanys-national-security-council-greenights-an-ai-safety-institute-modeled-after-the-uks-aisi/)*

**4. UK announces £1.1bn AI infrastructure package and £400m chip plan**

The UK government unveiled a £1.1bn AI infrastructure investment alongside a £400m sovereign compute and chip initiative, with the Prime Minister explicitly framing the package around encouraging British startups to "scale here" and "stay here." Industry commentary noted the plan represents a significant step but leaves key energy supply questions unanswered. The announcements coincide with Cosine being selected as the centrepiece of the UK's Sovereign AI initiative to develop Britain's first frontier model.

**5. Anthropic releases Claude Mythos 5 and Fable 5 with tiered access model**

Anthropic released Claude Mythos 5 exclusively to trusted organisations (including cyber partners) and Claude Fable 5 to the public, with the latter carrying restrictions designed to prevent use in cyberattacks. The release was accompanied by an Anthropic study showing its Mythos Preview model can convert security patches into working exploits within hours—before auto-updates reach a single device—effectively arguing that the old patch rhythm is obsolete. The tiered release strategy and accompanying safety claims subsequently attracted the US government enforcement action described above.

**6. Landmark German ruling holds Google directly liable for AI Overview content**

A German regional court ruled that Google is directly liable for false statements made in its AI-generated search overviews, finding that existing limited liability protections for search engine operators do not extend to AI-generated content. In the case at issue, Google's AI falsely linked two publishers to fraud using claims absent from any linked source. The ruling is widely assessed as a potential global precedent for AI-generated content liability.

**10. China's $295bn AI infrastructure buildout targets 80% domestic chip content**

China plans to invest approximately $295bn in a nationwide AI data centre network over five years, with at least 80% of technology sourced from domestic suppliers including Huawei, effectively locking out US suppliers. Taiwan is separately considering criminalising AI chip smuggling to China for the first time. The scale of the buildout, combined with US guardrail concerns flagged earlier in the week, reinforces the urgency of the US-China AI competition dynamic.
