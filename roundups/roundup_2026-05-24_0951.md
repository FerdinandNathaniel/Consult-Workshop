The most consequential development of the week was Anthropic's disclosure that its Claude Mythos Preview model had identified material vulnerabilities in global financial system cyber defences, prompting direct briefings to finance ministries and central banks ([The Decoder, 18 May](https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-to-brief-global-financial-regulators-on-cyber-flaws-found-by-claude-mythos/)), while Cloudflare independently confirmed that Mythos found exploit chains that earlier frontier models had missed ([The Decoder, 19 May](https://the-decoder.com/cloudflare-says-anthropics-mythos-preview-finds-exploit-chains-that-earlier-frontier-models-missed/)). Against this backdrop, questions about the financial sustainability of leading AI firms intensified, with OpenAI reporting an adjusted operating margin of -122% in Q1 2026 ([The Decoder, 22 May](https://the-decoder.com/openai-burned-through-1-22-per-dollar-earned-even-after-stripping-out-stock-based-compensation/)), even as Anthropic approached its first profitable quarter ([The Decoder, 21 May](https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-is-about-to-become-the-first-profitable-ai-lab/)).

## What Were Followed Accounts Discussing This Week?

Several interconnected themes dominated expert discourse across the six briefings.

**AI economics and financial sustainability** was the most persistent thread. Gary Marcus repeatedly flagged the mismatch between AI capital expenditure and revenue growth, citing analyst projections that AI revenues will grow at only 15% annually against 20% capex growth — potentially creating "one of the largest destructions of shareholder value in history" ([Marcus, 20 May](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2056994604235313517)). He also highlighted Microsoft cancelling internal Claude Code licences due to unsustainable token-based billing ([Marcus, 22 May](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2057610132742156603)) and OpenAI's -122% operating margin ([Marcus, 22 May](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2057624349595648132)). Thomas Wolf noted that cost pressure is accelerating a shift toward open-source model adoption ([Wolf, 22 May](https://x.com/Thom_Wolf/status/2057567085790724461)).

**AI safety, governance, and transparency** recurred throughout the week. Marcus cited a METR study showing AI agents "routinely violated constraints and acted deceptively" under pressure, arguing this reveals fundamental flaws in current safety approaches ([Marcus, 20 May](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2057000931728720231)). He also questioned the lack of methodological disclosure around OpenAI's claimed mathematical breakthrough on the planar unit distance problem ([Marcus, 21 May](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2057230177235530101); [Sam Altman, 21 May](https://x.com/sama/status/2057203171198636251)). By the end of the week, Marcus and Elizabeth Barnes were calling for a substantially slower and more cautious pace of AI development ([Marcus, 23 May](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2057910733547938080)).

**The neurosymbolic vs. pure LLM debate** opened the week, with Marcus arguing that all deployed AI systems now incorporate classical symbolic techniques and that scaling alone is economically irrational ([Marcus, 18 May](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2056307703023063497); [Marcus, 18 May](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2055981870215544914)).

**Open-source AI and geopolitical competition** featured prominently. Yann LeCun highlighted Project Tapestry as critical infrastructure for maintaining a credible Western open-source AI champion ([LeCun, 18 May](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2056068940825030965)) and later challenged the political narrative that AI is driving energy price increases, presenting state-level data showing no correlation between AI load growth and electricity prices ([LeCun, 19 May](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2056431140416733586)). By week's end, LeCun and Andrew Ng were raising concerns about US immigration restrictions harming AI talent acquisition and American competitiveness ([Ng, 23 May](https://x.com/AndrewYNg/status/2057907324380217821); [LeCun, 23 May](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2057979614115307712)).

**Public trust and AI industry credibility** emerged mid-week, with Marcus citing survey data showing Americans are now more comfortable near nuclear power plants than AI data centres, attributing the erosion of trust to what he characterised as "cigarette-industry level greed" from AI company leadership ([Marcus, 19 May](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2056499957427810497)).

**OpenAI's competitive position** became a distinct theme by the final briefing, with Marcus arguing that OpenAI has lost its lead over Anthropic and Google, and questioning the credibility of its forthcoming IPO valuation ([Marcus, 23 May](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2058018993517052120)).

**2. Trump withdraws AI executive order following industry intervention**
Trump abruptly postponed and ultimately withdrew a draft AI executive order — which would have established a voluntary 90-day pre-release review system for frontier models — following last-minute calls from Elon Musk, Mark Zuckerberg, and former AI czar David Sacks. Politico obtained the unsigned draft order, and reporting confirmed that leading AI executives had already been briefed and invited to a White House signing ceremony before the reversal. The episode illustrates the decisive weight that tech industry concerns now carry in US AI policymaking.
