## Summary of the Week

Nvidia's mobilisation of over $500 billion in AI infrastructure financing—backed by Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs, and KKR, with Nvidia itself guaranteeing up to 25% of residual hardware value—represents the most structurally significant development of the week, creating contingent liabilities on the chipmaker's balance sheet and prompting the Bank of England to flag potential financial stability risks from concentrated AI sector exposure ([source](https://the-decoder.com/nvidia-guarantees-its-own-chips-value-to-unlock-500-billion-in-ai-infrastructure-financing/)). Concerns about circular financing arrangements, speculative valuations, and the concentration of 87.5% of VC funding in AI compounded the systemic risk picture, with analysts drawing comparisons to Enron-style financial engineering and questioning whether AI revenues justify the scale of capital expenditure ([source](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2087546612662890532)). On the regulatory front, the EU moved to mandate watermarking of AI-generated content with major technology firms already complying ([source](https://www.politico.eu/article/artificial-intelligence-ai-watermark-big-tech/)), while the White House signalled an expansion of its AI policy framework to cover open-source models ([source](https://www.wired.com/story/the-white-house-is-going-to-expand-its-ai-policy/)). OpenAI's internal governance deteriorated visibly, with at least nine senior departures—including heads of ethics, safety, and communications—raising questions about organisational stability ahead of anticipated capital market activity in the AI sector ([source](https://www.wired.com/story/openai-safety-security-ai-agents-culture/)).

## What Were Followed Accounts Discussing This Week?

Followed accounts—predominantly Gary Marcus, Yann LeCun, Andrew Ng, Thomas Wolf, and Sam Altman—converged on several recurring themes across the week, with the tone shifting progressively from technical debate toward systemic economic and governance concern.

**AI infrastructure financing and bubble risk** dominated from mid-week onward. Early in the week, discussion centred on Nvidia's novel GPU-as-asset-class financing model ([source](https://the-decoder.com/nvidia-guarantees-its-own-chips-value-to-unlock-500-billion-in-ai-infrastructure-financing/)), but by Wednesday and Thursday, Gary Marcus was amplifying warnings about circular financing diagrams, Enron-style revenue inflation, and the systemic banking risk posed by interconnected Big Tech, private equity, and AI company balance sheets ([source](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2087546612662890532), [source](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2087655243505136000)). The concentration of 87.5% of VC funding in AI—at the expense of biotech and clean energy—was flagged as a structural vulnerability for the broader Western economy ([source](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2087667610792194232)).

**OpenAI's organisational instability** emerged as a sustained thread from Monday through Friday. Initial commentary focused on departures from safety and ethics functions ([source](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2086978679822028962)); by Thursday and Friday, the narrative had broadened to include the COO, CPO, and CCO, with Marcus drawing explicit WeWork parallels and questioning whether the departures undermined investor confidence in near-term AGI claims ([source](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2088374104013562217), [source](https://www.wired.com/story/openai-safety-security-ai-agents-culture/)).

**Open-weight versus open-source AI governance** was a prominent theme early in the week, triggered by Meta's release of Muse Glimmer. Gary Marcus criticised media conflation of the two concepts and highlighted the opacity of proprietary training processes even in ostensibly open models ([source](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2086837786116841715)), while Yann LeCun framed Meta's strategy as a competitive reorientation toward distribution scale rather than model quality ([source](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2086973093713457510)). This debate fed directly into the White House's reported plans to extend AI regulation to open models ([source](https://www.wired.com/story/the-white-house-is-going-to-expand-its-ai-policy/)).

**AI security and dual-use risks** recurred throughout the week: the first documented autonomous AI attack on OpenAI's model via Hugging Face ([source](https://x.com/Thom_Wolf/status/2086384005910315282)), a prompt-injection vulnerability in Atlassian's Rovo agent ([source](https://the-decoder.com/hidden-text-in-a-pdf-is-enough-to-steal-sensitive-data-through-atlassians-ai-agent-rovo/)), leaked passwords found in ChatGPT's hidden reasoning traces ([source](https://the-decoder.com/but-marinade-and-leaked-passwords-are-what-researchers-found-in-chatgpts-hidden-reasoning/)), and near-perfect LLM prompt reverse-engineering ([source](https://the-decoder.com/researchers-can-now-reverse-engineer-llm-prompts-from-output-text-with-near-perfect-accuracy/)) collectively illustrated an accelerating attack surface for AI-integrated enterprise systems.

**AI economic viability** sharpened as a concern by Friday, with Marcus citing an OpenAI internal study showing no correlation between employee AI usage and earnings, and noting $3 trillion in capex with "zero correlation with value creation" ([source](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2088235919187079608), [source](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2088352808995008940)). Slow adoption of Anthropic's most powerful model due to pricing concerns ([source](https://the-decoder.com/fable-5s-slow-adoption-suggests-corporate-willingness-to-pay-for-frontier-ai-has-hit-a-ceiling/)) reinforced the view that corporate willingness to pay for frontier AI may have reached a ceiling.

**European AI sovereignty and regulation** formed a quieter but consistent thread: Mistral's initiative to build one gigawatt of European compute by 2030 ([source](https://x.com/arthurmensch/status/2088000058910199921)), the EU's mandatory AI watermarking regime ([source](https://www.politico.eu/article/artificial-intelligence-ai-watermark-big-tech/)), and Mistral's new EU data residency options ([source](https://the-decoder.com/mistral-now-offers-eu-data-processing-and-priority-access-but-both-come-with-important-limits/)) all pointed toward a European regulatory and industrial strategy taking concrete shape.

## Most Relevant Items

**1. Nvidia's $500 billion AI infrastructure financing guarantee**
Nvidia is mobilising over $500 billion in AI infrastructure financing through a consortium of Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs, and KKR, guaranteeing up to 25% of the residual value of its own hardware to de-risk investor participation. This arrangement transfers asset depreciation risk back to the chipmaker, creating a contingent liability on Nvidia's balance sheet. The Bank of England has already flagged potential financial stability risks should the AI sector experience a significant downturn, making this a material item for systemic risk monitoring.

**2. Circular AI financing, speculative valuations, and systemic economic risk**
Analysts and followed accounts documented circular financing arrangements in which Big Tech, private equity, and AI companies inflate apparent revenues through interconnected deals, with comparisons drawn to Enron-style financial engineering. OpenAI and Anthropic were described as "massively unprofitable," with much of their spending recycled back as revenue from Big Tech partners. The concentration of 87.5% of VC funding in AI was flagged as a structural risk that starves other sectors—including biotech and clean energy—of capital.

**3. White House to expand AI policy framework to open-source models**
The White House is preparing to extend its AI regulatory framework to encompass open-source and open-weight models, according to WIRED, signalling a shift toward more comprehensive governance after an initial period of regulatory restraint. The move reflects growing recognition that existing policy—focused primarily on closed, proprietary systems—leaves a significant oversight gap as open models proliferate. Specific mechanisms and stringency of any new requirements remain unclear.

**4. EU mandatory AI watermarking enters force**
The European Union is implementing mandatory watermarking requirements for AI-generated content, with major technology firms already complying. The measure establishes a regulatory precedent for disclosure obligations that were previously voluntary or absent, and may serve as a template for other jurisdictions. Anthropic separately announced global watermarking of all Claude outputs using the C2PA standard, with detection tools available for third-party verification.
