## Summary of the Week

Anthropic's $65 billion Series H round at a $965 billion valuation ([The Decoder, 28 May](https://the-decoder.com/claude-company-anthropic-nears-a-trillion-dollar-valuation-after-raising-65-billion-in-series-h/)) crystallised the central tension of the week: record capital flows into AI coexist with mounting evidence that enterprise returns are disappointing, valuations may be detached from fundamentals, and regulatory frameworks are struggling to keep pace with deployment. ## What Were Followed Accounts Discussing This Week?

Followed accounts—dominated by Gary Marcus, with contributions from Yann LeCun and Thomas Wolf—returned repeatedly to a cluster of interconnected concerns about AI economics, market integrity, and governance.

**AI bubble risk and valuation disconnect** was the most persistent theme across the week. Marcus highlighted Google CEO Sundar Pichai's own acknowledgement of "irrationality in the current AI boom" ([25 May](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2058706349870203344)) and amplified financial journalist Andrew Ross Sorkin's prediction of an inevitable market crash ([25 May](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2058710334941311085)). By mid-week, Marcus was citing Michael Burry's warning that the OpenAI, Anthropic, and SpaceX IPOs alone could extract more capital than the entire 2000 tech bubble ([28 May](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2059722562066874769)). By the end of the week, the focus had sharpened to commoditisation risk: Chinese AI alternatives offering comparable capabilities at a fraction of the cost were described as potentially existential for premium-priced Western models ([29 May](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2060186969276387341)), while concrete corporate cost-cutting—Microsoft, Uber, Fortune 500 executives demanding reductions—was presented as evidence that the "tokenmaxxing" era is ending ([29–30 May](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2060194655292309696)).

**Market integrity and index rule changes** emerged as a related concern. Marcus characterised NASDAQ's rewriting of index inclusion rules to enable the SpaceX IPO—eliminating the 10% free float minimum and cutting the seasoning period from up to 12 months to 15 trading days—as "brazen retail fleecing" with direct implications for pension and retirement savings ([26 May](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2059024052380115032)). SpaceX's $1 trillion IPO valuation despite $13 billion in losses since 2023 drew further scrutiny ([27 May](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2059391566981058905)).

**AI capability limitations and quality degradation** were discussed throughout the week. Demis Hassabis was cited stating that today's systems are "nowhere near AGI" ([25 May](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2058713257167429655)); AI code generation was described as producing "slop" even by advocates ([25 May](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2058673424449597879)); Starbucks' failed AI inventory system was cited as a concrete deployment failure ([28 May](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2059854118060257638)); and by week's end, research showing ChatGPT diagnosed 40 million users with a fictional disease was used to illustrate systemic hallucination risks at scale ([30 May](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2060543247437668574)). A parallel thread on AI-hallucinated citations appearing in biomedical papers at twelve times the pre-2023 rate reinforced concerns about scientific integrity ([The Decoder, 26 May](https://the-decoder.com/ai-hallucinated-citations-are-creeping-into-papers-that-shape-clinical-guidelines-researchers-warn/)).

**Technical course correction toward neurosymbolic and hybrid AI** was a counterpoint offered by both Marcus and LeCun. Marcus argued that recent advances—including Claude Code and DeepMind's mathematical reasoning systems—validate the case for hybrid approaches over pure scaling ([27–28 May](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2059659537615872326)). LeCun highlighted OpenAI's disproof of an Erdős conjecture and Meta's ATLAS project formalising mathematics in Lean 4 as evidence of genuine progress in verifiable AI reasoning ([29 May](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2060101758165123472)).

**Public and institutional sentiment** also shifted visibly during the week. Wall Street Journal reporting on growing negative American attitudes toward AI was cited ([25 May](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2058688527760847141)), and Pope Leo XIV's encyclical *Magnifica Humanitas*—calling for AI restraint and warning of power concentration—generated significant commentary, including a Silicon Valley backlash ([Politico EU, 29 May](http://politico.com/news/magazine/2026/05/29/pope-leo-ai-jd-vance-00938737)). Uber's COO reporting disappointing AI returns despite rising costs was flagged as a potential inflection point for market confidence ([26 May](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2059058176075420111)).

**3. Illinois passes America's strongest AI safety bill**
Illinois lawmakers passed legislation requiring companies including OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google to obtain third-party verification that they are meeting safety standards. The law represents the most stringent US state-level AI safety framework to date and may set a precedent for federal or other state-level action, though its interaction with sector-specific financial regulation remains unaddressed.
