*Based on 6 daily briefings · 22 June 2026 to 27 June 2026*

## Summary of the Week

The most consequential development of the week was the emergence of a de facto US government licensing regime for frontier AI models: OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol was restricted to customer-by-customer government approval ([The Decoder, 26 Jun](https://the-decoder.com/openais-gpt-5-6-rollout-now-requires-us-government-approval-on-a-customer-by-customer-basis/)), while the Trump administration partially lifted Anthropic's export ban on its Mythos 5 model for a select group of US organisations, leaving a second advanced model—Fable—still blocked ([Politico, 27 Jun](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/06/26/white-house-makes-peace-with-anthropic-for-now-00965675)). OpenAI's CEO publicly characterised the arrangement as "unsustainable," signalling that major AI labs view these controls as economically inefficient and potentially precedent-setting ([The Decoder, 26 Jun](https://the-decoder.com/openais-claude-mythos-competitor-gpt-5-6-sol-launches-under-government-controlled-access-it-calls-unsustainable/)). In parallel, European policymakers moved in the opposite direction—toward proactive infrastructure sovereignty—with stakeholders arguing that controlling the entire AI stack from hardware to software is essential for competitive resilience ([Sifted, 22 Jun](https://sifted.eu/articles/owning-the-entire-ai-stack-is-essential-brnd/)), and cities being urged to set binding terms for the AI and data centre boom before regulatory arbitrage takes hold ([Politico EU, 23 Jun](https://www.politico.eu/sponsored-content/cities-must-set-the-terms-for-the-ai-infrastructure-boom/)). Cutting across both geographies, expert commentary throughout the week raised persistent concerns about AI investment overvaluation, with Goldman Sachs analysis cited as showing markets may have priced in up to $17 trillion in excess AI value ([Gary Marcus, 24 Jun](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2069395963144487105)), and open-source model releases from Chinese developers further eroding the competitive moats underpinning Big Tech's infrastructure spending thesis.

## What Were Followed Accounts Discussing This Week?

Followed accounts—dominated by Gary Marcus and, to a lesser extent, Yann LeCun—maintained a remarkably consistent critical posture across the week, with several themes intensifying as new policy events unfolded.

**AI valuation and capital misallocation** was the most persistent theme. Early in the week, Marcus flagged Goldman Sachs analysis suggesting markets have priced in $27 trillion in AI value against an estimated $10–22 trillion in actual discounted value, implying a potential $5–17 trillion bubble ([Gary Marcus, 24 Jun](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2069395963144487105)). By Thursday, the framing had sharpened: Marcus characterised hyperscaling investments as "the greatest capital misallocation in history," drawing an analogy to 19th-century railway busts ([Gary Marcus, 26 Jun](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2070355448629264769)). On Friday, the competitive threat from lower-cost Chinese open-source models—including Zhipu's GLM-5.2 under MIT licence—was cited as further undermining the trillion-dollar infrastructure spending thesis ([George Noble via Gary Marcus, 25 Jun](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2070004313091715264)).

**AI hype and CEO credibility** ran in parallel throughout the week. Monday's briefing featured concerns about big tech hiding the true costs of AI and warnings of a potential AI winter driven by over-promising ([Gary Marcus, 22 Jun](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2068861819142517200)). By Tuesday, the critique had shifted to AI company CEOs sounding "disconnected from reality" and overstating AI's impact outside narrow domains like software engineering ([Gary Marcus, 23 Jun](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2069147559537607092); [Kareem Carr via Gary Marcus, 23 Jun](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2069126771707187579)).

**US AI governance and regulatory opacity** became the dominant theme from Thursday onward, tracking real-world events. Marcus criticised the White House's de facto regulation of frontier models—including GPT-5.6 delays—as creating "the worst of all possible worlds": regulatory control without transparent criteria ([Gary Marcus, 26 Jun](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2070257533076582550)). By Friday, the partial lifting of Anthropic's export ban on Mythos 5 while Fable remained blocked drew characterisation as "more arbitrariness, more favouritism" ([Gary Marcus, 27 Jun](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2070669047960940977)), with the broader argument that deregulatory advocates had left the US government unprepared to craft coherent AI policy ([Gary Marcus, 27 Jun](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2070632952976040419)).

**AI labour market and economic shock** emerged mid-week and persisted. Anthropic's own warning that it no longer needs junior engineers—and that an economic shock will follow when other industries replicate this—was amplified ([The Decoder, 26 Jun](https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-doesnt-need-junior-engineers-anymore-thanks-to-ai-and-warns-of-an-economic-shock-when-other-industries-follow/)). Polling data cited on Friday suggested rare bipartisan consensus that corporations should be taxed to fund AI job displacement programmes rather than receiving subsidies ([David Shor via Gary Marcus, 27 Jun](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2070561075857656393)).

**Open-source AI and national competitiveness** was a recurring but more constructive thread. LeCun argued early in the week that China's open-source AI lead positions it for potential general AI leadership by 2026–2030 ([Yann LeCun, 22 Jun](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2068838532630687950)), and highlighted India's participation in Project Tapestry as a model for sovereign multilingual AI development ([Yann LeCun, 22 Jun](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2068732746013241383)). The theme connected to European discussions about rebuilding the tech stack and owning the full AI infrastructure chain.

**Environmental and transparency demands** appeared mid-week, with UN Secretary-General Guterres calling for mandatory public disclosure of AI data centres' land, water, and power consumption, framing hidden costs as an equity issue ([Gary Marcus, 24 Jun](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2069474251820974556)).
