The US midterm elections delivered a further political shock to the sector, with voters rejecting data centre proposals in 40 out of 41 local ballot measures, while the Bank of England reported that AI spending increases company expenditure without generating measurable worker productivity gains—directly undermining the macroeconomic case for continued capital deployment at current scale ([data centre ballot results](https://the-decoder.com/after-midterm-rout-openai-tries-to-win-over-communities-it-needs-for-ai-expansion/); [BoE productivity finding](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2118627785027805220)).

## What Were Followed Accounts Discussing This Week?

Followed accounts—dominated by Gary Marcus, with contributions from Yann LeCun, Thomas Wolf, and Sam Altman—coalesced around four interlocking themes that intensified across the week.

**AI valuation and financial sustainability** was the dominant and most persistent thread. Early in the week, discussion centred on Grok Bot's admission that serving costs exceeded revenue and Anthropic's deteriorating unit economics—compute bills quintupling in a year, Q3 revenue growth (47% QoQ) outpaced by infrastructure cost growth (78% QoQ), and total commitments reaching $895 billion ([Grok Bot cost issues](https://the-decoder.com/spacexsi-apparently-cuts-grok-bot-quotas-weeks-after-musk-called-it-his-fastest-growing-product/); [Anthropic financials](https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-grows-revenue-twelvefold-but-posts-23-billion-loss/)). By mid-week, Marcus was characterising the sector as "the biggest misallocation of capital in history" and drawing explicit parallels to the dot-com fibre bubble. By Friday, the conversation had sharpened into a specific valuation crisis: Anthropic's IPO trajectory from $2 trillion to below $1 trillion, OpenAI's simultaneous $11 billion raise at $1.25 trillion requiring wealthy private investors ("dentists") as institutional appetite saturated, and S-1 language explicitly admitting unsustainable losses ([OpenAI funding](https://the-decoder.com/openai-lands-an-11-billion-funding-round-but-has-to-court-dentists-for-more-capital/); [Anthropic IPO resistance](https://the-decoder.com/anthropics-1-5-trillion-ipo-hits-resistance-as-investors-balk-at-valuation/)).

**The scaling hypothesis under challenge** ran as a technical counterpoint throughout the week. LeCun highlighted that smaller models (109M parameters) can outperform 72B-parameter models, endorsed LeVJEPA 1.5 Large achieving comparable performance with 5.6x less compute, and amplified Thomas Wolf's showcase of "weight-space programming" achieving 6x math improvement by editing only 0.016% of model weights. Wolf also documented that 18 of 20 leading open models now use Mixture-of-Experts architecture, with Ricursive's Ripple-65B trained for $32M—one-tenth prior state-of-the-art cost. These developments collectively challenge the capital-intensive scaling narrative that has underpinned trillion-dollar valuations.

**AI governance and regulatory credibility** emerged as a third recurring theme. Marcus flagged the Bank of England's finding that AI spending raises costs without productivity gains as "bad news for the GenAI bubble." The EU AI Act's watermarking requirements were shown to be bypassable on older Claude models via a single parameter change, with Anthropic responding only with a compliance warning rather than a technical fix ([watermark bypass](https://the-decoder.com/anthropics-text-watermark-is-easy-to-bypass-on-several-older-claude-models/)). METR's audit finding that Anthropic may have crossed its own internal AI acceleration safety threshold in July—with the company redacting key evidence—added to concerns about the gap between stated governance commitments and practice ([METR audit](https://the-decoder.com/metr-finds-anthropic-may-have-crossed-its-own-ai-acceleration-warning-threshold-back-in-july/)).

**Political economy of AI infrastructure** crystallised sharply after the midterms. Marcus framed the ballot-box rejection of data centres as the first time ordinary Americans had wielded leverage over Silicon Valley priorities, while simultaneously warning of potential corporate bailouts framed as national security imperatives. OpenAI's rapid post-election commitment to drop NDAs with public officials on data centre projects was noted as a reactive rather than principled policy shift ([OpenAI NDA commitment](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1028454/openai-data-center-nda)).

A fundamental disagreement has emerged between France and the United States over multilateral AI safety oversight within the G7 framework, with the White House rejecting the French proposal as "unnecessary bureaucracy." ### 2. UK AI Economic Impact Projections Revised Sharply Downward
The UK AI minister has conceded that the government's headline £400 billion economic impact figure—which originated primarily from Google analysis—overstates the benefit by more than two-thirds, with researchers attributing the shortfall to slower-than-anticipated adoption rates. This represents a significant credibility problem for AI-driven economic policy and raises questions about the reliability of AI impact assessments used to justify public investment and regulatory trade-offs.
