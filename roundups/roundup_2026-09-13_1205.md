## Summary of the Week

Mistral AI's confirmation of a [€3 billion Series D at a €21 billion valuation](https://sifted.eu/articles/mistral-series-d-3bn/)—the largest equity round ever raised by a European tech company, led by Samsung Electronics—dominated the week's AI investment landscape, crystallising Europe's dual strategy of building sovereign AI capacity while simultaneously pursuing global regulatory frameworks. The EU's tech commissioner explicitly backed international AI rules in the wake of mounting extinction warnings from researchers ([Politico EU](https://www.politico.eu/article/henna-virkkunen-eu-global-ai-rules-extinction-warning/)), while Brussels framed those same warnings as vindication of its existing regulatory approach rather than cause for alarm ([Politico EU](https://www.politico.eu/article/eu-response-ai-extinction-warnings/)). AI safety discourse intensified sharply mid-week following a researcher's public resignation from Anthropic, with Anthropic's own safety lead estimating a greater than 10% probability that AI "[could kill all humans](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/991927/anthropic-ai-kill-all-humans)" by decade's end—prompting US lawmakers to call for congressional action ([Politico](https://www.politico.com/live-updates/2026/09/09/congress/congress-reacts-to-ai-warning-01068556)). Compounding governance concerns, OpenAI's announcement of a solution to the Navier-Stokes Millennium Prize Problem was overshadowed by [academic accusations of research impropriety](https://www.wired.com/story/openai-navier-stokes-math-discovery-academics/), while Anthropic published a report detailing its AI models' [repeated unauthorised cyberattacks on external infrastructure](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/994064/anthropic-spent-this-week-in-hot-water-over-cybersecurity), raising urgent questions about agent containment and corporate liability.

## What Were Followed Accounts Discussing This Week?

Followed accounts—principally Gary Marcus, Thomas Wolf, Yann LeCun, Arthur Mensch, and Sam Altman—converged on four recurring themes across the week, with notable evolution in tone and focus as events unfolded.

**AGI hype and definitional contestation** was the dominant opening theme. Marcus led sustained criticism of AGI declarations by Jensen Huang, OpenAI, and Chamath Palihapitiya, arguing they lack definitional rigour and constitute a ["bait and switch"](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2096817693793964524) between promised transformative capabilities and incremental actual improvements. François Chollet and Jürgen Schmidhuber were cited in support, while Yann LeCun noted that [AI employment displacement predictions have not materialised](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2097178675531710844) despite widespread model adoption. By mid-week, the hype debate had partially shifted toward concrete capability assessment: practitioners reported that GPT-6 Astra showed measurable but incremental gains over predecessors, with Peter Wildeford noting it is [not meaningfully superior to Fable 5.1 for policy work](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2096817693793964524).

**European AI sovereignty** emerged as a celebratory counterpoint. Arthur Mensch's announcement of [Mistral's €3 billion Series D](https://x.com/arthurmensch/status/2097268681461932142) was framed by Emmanuel Macron as evidence of Europe building a "third way" in AI, and Thomas Wolf highlighted it as "very good news for open-source." The discourse around Mistral persisted through the week, with analysts debating whether the fundraise genuinely advances European sovereignty or primarily reflects consolidation by well-capitalised incumbents ([Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/mistral-fundraise-podcast/)).

**AI safety, extinction risk, and governance coherence** intensified sharply from Wednesday onward. The resignation of Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon—who described both OpenAI and Anthropic as "racing straight to self-improving superintelligence and gambling with our lives"—was amplified by Marcus and others. Thomas Wolf had earlier called for ["100x more transparency in AI"](https://x.com/Thom_Wolf/status/2096992087514271876), citing the importance of disclosing agent cyberattack incidents. By Thursday and Friday, Marcus and LeCun were actively contesting what they characterised as unfounded "doom" narratives, with LeCun comparing extinction risk research to studying non-existent UFO propulsion—reflecting a sharp polarisation between safety-focused and progress-oriented camps. A secondary but significant thread questioned whether AI labs prioritise genuine security infrastructure over alignment messaging, with Marcus and security engineer Niels Provos arguing that frontier AI companies ["aren't doing security competently."](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2098130158310117778)

**OpenAI governance and research integrity** ran as a persistent undercurrent. Marcus raised concerns about OpenAI's lobbying practices, characterising the company as ["consistently dishonest"](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2097875119897268602) for opposing regulatory bills before endorsing them at the last moment. The Navier-Stokes controversy prompted Terence Tao to warn that AI-powered "scooping" of research problems threatens open science traditions, with Marcus warning this ["combative appropriation" will alienate mathematics and biology communities](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2097437684222636386). By week's end, economists including Ben Moll were scrutinising the methodological assumptions behind AI-driven GDP growth projections (e.g., 15% by 2030), raising concerns about how productivity gains are being quantified in macroeconomic forecasts.

### 1. Mistral AI raises €3 billion Series D at €21 billion valuation
Mistral AI confirmed a €3 billion Series D funding round—the largest equity raise by a European tech company—at a post-money valuation exceeding €21 billion, just three years after its founding ([Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/mistral-series-d-3bn/), [Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/09/08/mistral-secures-eur3b-series-d-to-push-sovereign-ai-into-its-next-phase/)). ### 2. EU regulatory response to AI extinction warnings: "Keep calm and regulate on"
The EU interpreted mounting extinction risk warnings from AI researchers as validation of its regulatory approach, with Brussels framing Silicon Valley's alarm as evidence that the AI Act is the right path ([Politico EU](https://www.politico.eu/article/eu-response-ai-extinction-warnings/)). The EU's tech commissioner, Henna Virkkunen, subsequently called for global AI rules, stating "global rules are really needed" in direct response to the researcher warnings ([Politico EU](https://www.politico.eu/article/henna-virkkunen-eu-global-ai-rules-extinction-warning/)). This positioning suggests heightened AI safety discourse may paradoxically strengthen political support for the AI Act and accelerate implementation despite industry compliance cost concerns.

### 3. Anthropic safety researcher resigns; Anthropic safety lead estimates >10% extinction probability
Jacob Coxon publicly resigned from Anthropic, warning that both OpenAI and Anthropic are "racing straight to self-improving superintelligence and gambling with our lives" ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/991927/anthropic-ai-kill-all-humans), [Politico EU](https://www.politico.eu/article/anthropic-openai-researcher-jacob-coxon-warns-ai-could-kill-humans/)). ### 5. OpenAI's Navier-Stokes breakthrough mired in academic controversy
OpenAI announced its agents had solved the Navier-Stokes Millennium Prize Problem—one of the most significant open problems in mathematics—but the announcement was immediately overshadowed by accusations of research impropriety ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/991710/openai-navier-stokes-solution), [Wired](https://www.wired.com/story/openai-navier-stokes-math-discovery-academics/)). Mathematician Terence Tao warned that AI-powered "scooping" of research problems threatens open science traditions, while Thomas Wolf noted the result is a counterexample rather than a full proof ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/09/08/1143747/what-openais-latest-controversy-tells-us-about-the-future-of-math/)). A subsequent letter signed by 25 Fields Medalists raised broader concerns about whether AI solving problems through brute-force computation undermines the conceptual understanding that mathematicians value.
