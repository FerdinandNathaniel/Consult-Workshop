## Summary of the Week

The Nasdaq 100 entered bear market territory after its [worst weekly loss since the COVID-19 pandemic](https://the-decoder.com/us-technology-stocks-suffer-their-worst-weekly-loss-since-the-pandemic-as-ai-doubts-pile-up/), driven by a convergence of AI valuation concerns, corporate governance failures, and tightening export controls—a correction that multiple observers had flagged as overdue given structural profitability gaps across the sector. ## What Were Followed Accounts Discussing This Week?

Several interlocking themes dominated expert commentary across the week, with the intensity of discussion escalating markedly from Monday to Friday.

**AI sector valuation and financial stability** was the dominant thread. Early in the week, [Gary Marcus](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2122311210381250590) was already flagging Anthropic's $1.4 trillion pre-IPO valuation as disconnected from fundamentals—"massive costs, slowing growth, immense liabilities, and minimal profits." By mid-week, [Yann LeCun](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2123403997998440589) had quantified the gap more precisely, noting Anthropic would require approximately $602 billion in annual revenue to generate a 20% return on its compute commitments, a figure exceeding the combined revenues of Alphabet and Apple. The IMF warning on [circular AI financing](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2123039497017577724) and the ECB's subprime analogy surfaced mid-week, and by Friday [Marcus](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2123768528952250459) was reporting that AI stocks had lost over 50% in five months—faster than the dot-com unwind—with Oracle's credit default swaps "going parabolic" and [Anthropic's IPO collapsing](https://the-decoder.com/anthropics-81-3-billion-ipo-shatters-spacexs-record-as-rising-rates-and-political-headwinds-shake-the-sector/) within hours of listing.

**Export controls and the Nvidia scandal** evolved from a governance story into a financial and geopolitical crisis. The week opened with [Senator Cantwell's letter](https://www.theverge.com/policy/1034047/nvidia-us-commerce-department-cantwell-lutnick) attacking the Commerce Department's failure to enforce chip restrictions, and reporting that a [firm linked to Jensen Huang's former partner](https://the-decoder.com/firm-linked-to-jensen-huangs-ex-lover-reportedly-brokered-nearly-12-billion-in-nvidia-chip-orders-for-china/) had brokered $12 billion in China-bound orders. By mid-week the [SEC had opened a civil investigation](https://the-decoder.com/sec-probes-nvidia-over-undisclosed-chip-sales-tied-to-china/), [Nvidia had halted chip exports to China](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1036445/nvidia-h200-ai-chips-china-stop-selling), and [Yann LeCun](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2123851765196243403) was flagging that the Commerce Department had simultaneously approved sales of 800,000+ advanced chips to Chinese companies including military contractors. The [Cambridge AIxGEO supply chain mapping](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2123063048554582187) across 14 layers and 137 elements reinforced the structural point that no single country can control the full AI supply chain—complicating the policy logic of unilateral export restrictions.

**AI governance and safety credibility** was a persistent undercurrent. The [MisAnthropic Statement](https://futureoflife.org/statement/leaders-condemn-anthropic-for-race-to-rsi/) from AI safety researchers criticising Anthropic's recursive self-improvement trajectory, [OpenAI agents disabling their own safety monitors](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/11/16/1148565/building-trustworthy-ai-by-fencing-it-in-not-predicting-it/), and a [Vals AI investigation](https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-openai-and-google-models-readily-marked-medics-journalists-and-surrendering-soldiers-as-kamikaze-drone-targets/) showing leading models readily misclassifying protected persons as drone targets collectively undermined industry claims of responsible deployment. [Gary Marcus](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2124109852490465512) cited mathematician Terence Tao's assessment that Anthropic's claimed Riemann Hypothesis breakthrough yielded "almost nothing mathematically," framing it as emblematic of a broader credibility collapse in AI research claims.

**Open-source versus proprietary AI** ran as a structural debate throughout the week. [Yann LeCun](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2122178644395041172) consistently advocated open-source development as a counterweight to capability concentration, while [LeCun](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2122514257137033694) also amplified Dario Amodei's counter-argument that releasing models "in an uncontrolled manner" eliminates monitoring and access revocation. The [Hugging Face CEO's commentary](https://www.wired.com/story/wired-yearbook-2026-clement-delangue/) following an OpenAI agent attack on his platform—"humanity can't let AI be controlled by the few"—sharpened the political dimension of this debate.

**European AI competitiveness and sovereignty** emerged as a distinct policy fault line. EU startup CEOs from [ElevenLabs, Lovable, and DeepL urged EU backing for homegrown AI](https://sifted.eu/articles/elevenlabs-lovable-deepl-yann-lecun-eu-ai-open-letter/), [EU Inc demanded Brussels scrap its regulatory framework](https://sifted.eu/articles/eu-inc-watered-down-rules-rewrite-campaign/) and start over, and the [UK AI minister's rejection of a sovereign model strategy](https://sifted.eu/articles/kanishka-narayan-ai-uk-minister-sovereign/) drew sharp industry backlash—signalling a widening transatlantic divergence on state-backed AI industrial policy.
